# Dynamic int8 and picture rows

On LFM3-Omni-2B's decoder, dynamic int8 (ai-edge-quantizer's `dynamic_wi8_afp32`: int8 weights per channel, float activations that LiteRT's CPU path quantizes per row at run time) moved the hidden states at picture positions far from float32: cosine 0.571 at the worst position on the CPU (217 of 3,875 positions under 0.99), and 0.683 on the Mac GPU (LiteRT's Metal accelerator with float32 activations), where only the weights are int8. On the CPU one close text answer flipped ("purple" for "blue"; the float32 reference's top two tokens were 0.0485 apart in probability). LiteRT-LM's GPU generation on the Mac kept all 14 answers. The steps below found the cause and the form that shipped (the REPRODUCE.md entry "LFM3-Omni-2B").

1. **First measure bfloat16 against float32.** Run the decoder in bfloat16 (the checkpoint's dtype) and in float16 against float32 on the same sequences. Here bfloat16 reached cosine 0.962 at its worst position (7 positions under 0.99, all at pictures; mean 0.99982) and changed 2 of 233 teacher-forced tokens; float16, with the norms kept in float32, stayed at 0.999 or above. A bar of "every position ≥ 0.99" fails the checkpoint's dtype itself. The bar used here: the mean cosine over all positions at least the bfloat16 mean − 0.005 (0.99482); teacher-forced tokens ≥ 0.95 overall and ≥ 0.80 per prompt; free generation on the CPU and the GPU and the end-to-end checks all passing; the per-position minimum kept on record.
2. **Emulate the CPU kernel in PyTorch, so each try needs no export.** XNNPACK's dynamic int8 fully connected op multiplies int8 activations, quantized per row with a zero point, by int8 weights with one scale per output channel. The same product in PyTorch gave 0.569 on the sequence where the CPU measured 0.571, and 207 positions under 0.99 over all 23 sequences against 217 measured (its worst position, 0.41, was lower than any measured one, so it errs on the pessimistic side). Int8 weights with float activations gave 0.683, the Mac GPU's value. With the emulation, each group of fully connected layers can be switched between int8 and float in turn:
   - Putting any one group back to float left the worst picture position at 0.66 or lower: the error comes from every group, not from one layer or one op.
   - With only one group in int8, every group in the decoder layers but the attention gate projection pushed some picture positions under 0.99; the MLP down projection alone was the worst (0.758). Its input rows reach 87 times their RMS, and its outputs carried the largest error per layer (3.4 % on average at picture positions, against 0.7 % for the gate projection).
   - In the residual stream, text positions stayed at 0.95 or above through all 24 layers, while picture positions fell from layer 9 on (0.98 or above up to layer 8, 0.94 at layer 9, 0.45 at layer 14), and audio positions later and less (0.965 at layer 13, 0.840 at layer 14).
3. **Rescale per channel before quantizing (SmoothQuant's form).** At four places per layer — the input of the MLP down projection, the input of the short convolution's output projection (convolution layers only), and the outputs of the two norms — multiply column j of the weights that read channel j by s_j, and divide by s_j where the channel is made (the norm's weight, or the rows of the projection that make it). The float32 function stays the same up to rounding (cosine ≥ 0.99999999 against the original). s_j = amax_j^α / wmax_j^(1−α), clipped to [1e-4, 1e4]: amax_j is the largest |input_j| over 16 calibration sequences (6 with a picture, 5 transcriptions, 4 text only, 1 text-to-speech prompt; none of them a test prompt), wmax_j the largest |W[:, j]| of the weights that read the channel. In the emulation α = 0.65 at all four places did best (positions under 0.99: 207 → 162; worst position 0.41 → 0.67); α = 0.8 moved the error into the weights (448 positions under 0.99).

**Result.** The rescaled int8 decoder is the same size as plain int8 (1,790,502,512 bytes) and passed the bar on the CPU: mean cosine 0.9958, 230 of 233 teacher-forced tokens with both tokens of the close answer right, all 14 checked answers in free generation on the CPU and the GPU, every end-to-end check. Plain int8 missed it (mean 0.99464, one of the close answer's two tokens). Picture positions still sit well below bfloat16's values: on the CPU 159 positions under 0.99, the worst at 0.761 (the worst position per sequence: 0.761–0.972 with a picture, 0.835–0.895 with a picture and a spoken question, 0.985–0.994 with audio input, 0.969–0.990 with speech output, 0.993 or above with text only); on the Mac GPU at float32, 53 positions, the worst at 0.784. Float16 weights pass every position (0.999999 or above on the CPU) but make a 5.17 GB file, whose CPU generation took 16.9 GB of memory on the Mac and whose picture positions fall to 0.874 when the Mac GPU runs float16 activations.

The rescale script and the fitted scales: `lfm3omni_work/smooth_weights.py` and `lfm3omni_work/smooth_scales.npz` (one of the calibration pictures cannot be redistributed, so the scales are shipped instead of the calibration set).
