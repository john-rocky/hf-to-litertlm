# Reproducing d1-omni-600M-LiteRT

This guide rebuilds the twelve graphs, the host and the public check set of d1-omni-600M-LiteRT from the provider's checkpoint, checks them against the provider's own float32 code, and lists the results of the shipped files with the result file behind each number. The commands are in [`conversion/README.md`](conversion/README.md); this file gives what each step must produce.

## Sources

- The model: `LiquidAI/d1-omni-600M` at revision `414f8d6438174f5b2133a9c21a478fc42625e308` (LFM Open License v1.0). `model.safetensors` is 2,348,774,500 bytes, SHA-256 `0713bb05270c2685ad106522f4092bceeeb3a93cf79b401f399a712296c911e1`. The code files (`modeling_d1.py`, `encoder.py`, `vision.py`, `audio.py`, `prompt.py`), `config.json`, `tokenizer.json` and `tokenizer_config.json` are read at the same revision; `scripts/fetch_small.py` records their git blob ids and checks them. `tokenizer.json` ships unchanged (4,733,371 bytes).
- The public check set: 242 text records (3 examples from the model card, 14 written for this check set, 144 from SemIf under MIT, 61 MMLU records under MIT as the kev repository's transfer-v4 suite quotes them, one of them with a word changed as a control, and 20 generated by that suite under Apache-2.0; the licence files in `fixtures/` cover the last three), five CC0 photographs from Wikimedia Commons (page and author in each record, SHA-256 in each record's media entry) and six speech clips synthesised with Kokoro-82M (Apache-2.0) from scripts written for this check set. `scripts/media_fetch.py` downloads the photos and stops on any SHA-256 that differs from the record.

## Environments

Three Python environments, listed with their versions in `conversion/README.md`: the reference (the provider's code with PyTorch, 12 threads), the export (litert-torch 0.9.4, ai-edge-litert 2.2.0) and the host (ai-edge-litert 2.2.0, numpy, tokenizers, Pillow, soundfile; no PyTorch). The Mac was an Apple M4 Max with 128 GB; the phone a Samsung Galaxy S26 (SM-S942Q, 12 GB, Android 16).

## Steps

The twelve steps of `conversion/README.md`, with what each must produce:

1. Environment and source files: the source digests match; the host's tokenizer adapter gives the same token ids as `AutoTokenizer`.
2. Fixtures: `fixtures/requests.json`, the photos (SHA-256 checked), the speech clips and the encoded questions.
3. Reference: `ref/records_ref.json`, the provider's float32 probabilities, logits and media prefixes for every record.
4. Module checks: the graph module equals the provider in float64; the fp16-safe rewrite changes no float32 bit; `results/norm_range.json` gives each norm's largest sum of squares.
5. Decision graphs L128 to L4096: every file passes the criterion below on the Mac CPU and on Metal at float32.
6. Vision tower and projector: the host's crops equal the provider's preprocessing bit for bit; the tower is judged by its relative RMS against a float64 truth and end to end.
7. Audio encoders T501 to T3001: the host's log-mel is checked against the provider's (`audio_check.py --mel`); the prefixes are judged end to end.
8. Mac timing: `results/timing_mac_*.json`.
9. The repository: `ship/` and `results/ship_manifest.json`; `host/tests.py` passes and `host/verify.py --check-files` exits with 0.
10. The 5 s and 30 s audio buckets, which no reference record fills: the provider's float32 run of a 5 s clip and a 27.15 s clip, and the shipped files against it.
11. Galaxy S26, measurement app: the input rows, the legs of `device/plan_<round>.tsv` and their scores.
12. Galaxy S26, C-API runner: the NPU and GPU legs and their scores (the runner and the NPU runtime libraries are not included).

## Pass criterion

Every check compares post-temperature probabilities with the reference. A near tie is a question whose two most likely options in the reference are 0.02 or less apart. A file passes when it gives the reference's most likely option on every question that is not a near tie, the largest |Δp| is 0.02 or less, the mean |Δp| is 0.002 or less and no value is non-finite. The vision and audio prefixes are not held to that bar element by element: they are judged by the questions asked through them, and the vision tower also by its relative RMS against a float64 truth.

These are the C-API runner's numbers. The measurement app (Kotlin CompiledModel API) gave 39.8 ms on L128 and 75.4 ms on L256 at FP16_WITH_FP32_ACCUM. On the NPU (Qualcomm JIT through LiteRT 2.2.0), L128 takes 29.6 ms with max |Δp| 0.0167 and mean 0.00173 on its 101 gate rows, but on all 210 public L128 rows it reaches 0.038 on 4 rows (mean 0.0032, one argmax change outside near ties); L256 (41.4 ms, mean 0.00204), the vision tower and the T1001 audio encoder fall outside the bar there too, so the NPU is not a recommended path for this model. The shipped L4096 file has not run on the phone.

A later run (2026-10-09, the demo app on the Kotlin CompiledModel API, the Hub file) of all 210 public L128 text rows: FP16_WITH_FP32_ACCUM max |Δp| 0.040 on one row (`semif_46b7029b9a704138b77a/answer`, argmax unchanged, mean 0.0019; the same scores on five repeats), FP32 max 0.0019 on the same rows; L256 (43 rows) 0.0064 / 0.0012. The conversion lane's per-bucket tables above are its 101 to 108 gate rows. For a guaranteed 0.02 bound on Android use FP32; FP16_WITH_FP32_ACCUM trades a rare 0.04 deviation for speed.
